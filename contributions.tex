This paper makes two primary contributions. First, we propose \textsc{AutoCUSUM}, an automatic procedure that calibrates the reference value and decision limit of a CUSUM rule from a finite sample of normal scores, without anomaly labels or a user-specified target alarm rate. The calibrated rule converts incoming scores into binary anomaly labels, separating score generation from sequential decision making. Second, we evaluate this decision layer across different scoring models and datasets, comparing alternative decision rules using observation-level detection metrics, false-alarm episode counts, and detection delay.
