The challenge of converting anomaly scores into discrete labels has motivated a range of thresholding strategies under normal-only calibration \cite{outlier_thresholding}. Calibrating score thresholds, alarm limits, or target alarm rates on a separate labeled validation set avoids direct optimization on the test labels, but still introduces supervision and selects the operating point using known anomalies. The setting considered here instead requires calibrating the decision rule from normal data alone. Simple unsupervised approaches apply summary statistics to the score distribution, such as $k$-sigma rules \cite{threshold_counter_1} or high-percentile cutoffs \cite{iqr_counter_1}. However, these methods implicitly assume specific score distributions (often Gaussian), rely on arbitrary parameter choices, and are highly sensitive to how representative the calibration sample is of the system’s normal operating behavior. This issue is exacerbated in streaming settings where the long-term score distribution is unknown \emph{a priori}.
